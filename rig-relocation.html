Relocating a mining rig: balcony, garage, or a rented space

Sooner or later the rig outgrows the living room: noise, heat, a second set of cards. The classic options are the balcony, the garage, or a rented space — and each has its own traps. Let's go through them honestly, plus two forgotten questions that surface after the move: wiring and transport.

The short version (for the no-time-to-read)

  • The three enemies of a new location: humidity, dust, temperature. Judge a spot by them, not by square meters.
  • Condensation is the winter killer. Cold hardware powered on in humid air collects water like a glass out of the fridge.
  • A balcony solves noise but adds moisture and neighbor questions. A garage solves space but adds dust and security concerns.
  • Check wiring and network before the move, not with cards in hand: a dedicated line and a stable link are half the success.
  • Transport the rig disassembled or strapped down. A card rattling in its slot in a moving car is a lottery with an expensive prize.

The balcony: closest, but humid

A glazed balcony is the most popular first step: heat goes out, noise stays behind the door, the outlet is near. The traps:

  • humidity and swings. In spring and autumn a balcony lives in "fog — dry — fog" mode. Moisture plus dust makes a conductive film on the boards. Minimum: a closed rack with intake at the bottom and exhaust at the top — never an "open table by the window";
  • summer overheating. A glass balcony in the sun is a greenhouse; without exhaust it runs hotter than the room (solutions in the summer article);
  • winter condensation. On a cold balcony, do not shut the rig down for long: cooled hardware sweats when powered on. A running rig heats its own microclimate — a stopped one does not;
  • neighbors. An exhaust blowing into somebody else's window becomes a complaint sooner or later.

The garage and a rented space

A garage removes the noise and space questions, but brings its own:

  • dust. Garage dust is fine and abundant — intake filters and a blow-out schedule (maintenance) become mandatory, not optional;
  • moisture. An unheated garage in winter is outdoor humidity indoors. A running rig holds its microclimate with its own heat; a stopped one gets wet. Long winter shutdowns = take the cards home;
  • security. A rig worth serious money behind a single padlock is a bold decision. An alarm and a camera are part of the budget here;
  • wiring. Garage circuits are often weak and "wandering" — size the line with our article; surge protection (UPS and relays) is mandatory.

A rented space (storage, a mining colocation) — the same checklist plus the contract: who is responsible for access, power and force majeure.

Wiring, network and the move itself

  • The line first, the cards second. The outlet and breaker at the new site are verified before the move: a night of "rig idle because the breaker tripped" is a classic.
  • Network. A balcony makes do with good Wi-Fi; a garage often needs a cable or an LTE router — test the ping to the pool in advance (network article).
  • Transport. Ideally, remove the cards and carry them separately in antistatic bags. Too lazy to remove — strap every card down and drive slowly: the riser and the slot are not rated for potholes (on riser fragility — here).
  • The first boot at the new site — in daylight, supervised. Half an hour of watching temperatures and hashrate is cheaper than a midnight failure in unfamiliar surroundings.
