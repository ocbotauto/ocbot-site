Network difficulty: why revenue drops at the same hashrate

A familiar picture: the rig untouched, hashrate the same, temperatures normal — yet fewer coins arrive than last month. The first suspicion falls on the pool, the miner, "something broke". Usually nothing broke: the network difficulty went up. Let's cover what it is, why it almost always grows, and what you can actually do about it.

The short version (for the no-time-to-read)

  • Your coin revenue = your hashrate divided by the network's hashrate. The network grew — your slice shrank, even with a perfect rig.
  • Difficulty almost always follows price upward. A coin appreciates → more miners join → everyone gets less.
  • Track revenue in coins and in money separately. "Fewer coins" and "less money" are different stories with different causes.
  • The difficulty chart is an indicator like the price chart. Checking it weekly is normal, not paranoia.
  • The only thing you control is the numerator: your own hashrate and uptime. Everything else is weather.

What difficulty is, in plain terms

Every coin's network is built so that blocks are found on a schedule — say, once a minute — no matter how many miners are working. If twice as many miners join, the network simply makes the task twice as "hard", and the schedule holds. That self-adjusting parameter is difficulty.

The block reward, meanwhile, is fixed by the protocol. So the total daily "pie" is roughly constant, and your slice is your share of the global hashrate. A rig delivers a steady 600, the network grows from 200 Th/s to 240 — your coin revenue drops 17% without you touching a single setting. All fair: there are simply more eaters.

Why difficulty grows — and when it falls

Difficulty is a mirror of the crowd. The mechanics are simple:

  • price rises → mining pays better → new cards and ASICs join → difficulty chases the price, usually lagging by days to weeks;
  • price falls → the rigs with the most expensive power shut down → difficulty drops, and the survivors' share grows back a little (covered in the winter article);
  • new hardware → the launch of more efficient cards or devices raises network hashrate even without a price rally.

The key conclusion: "my rig mines X coins a day" is not a constant but a function of two variables. The second one is out of your hands.

What to do about it

  • Keep books in coins. Revenue in money is a product of two random variables; the coin figure shows the erosion of your share. If coins decline for months, that is difficulty, not a breakdown.
  • Compare coins against each other by revenue on your hardware, not by hype. That is what a calculator with an honest formula is for (how to count — here).
  • Hold the numerator. The only lever you have is your own hashrate and uptime: a stable overclock, minimal downtime, no hours of "lying silent".
  • Do not chase reconfigurations. Switching coins every week over difficulty moves means paying in downtime and fees for an imaginary gain.
